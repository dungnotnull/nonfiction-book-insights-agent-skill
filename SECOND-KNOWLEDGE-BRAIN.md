## Core Concepts & Frameworks

### SQ3R (Survey-Question-Read-Recite-Review)
- **Source**: Francis Pleasant Robinson, *Effective Study* (1946), Harper & Row
- **Purpose**: Structured method for textbook comprehension and retention
- **Application**: Survey (overview structure), Question (formulate questions), Read (actively answer questions), Recite (recall without looking), Review (consolidate)
- **Best for**: Standard comprehension, systematic book analysis, first-time deep reads

### Adler's Analytical Reading
- **Source**: Mortimer Adler, *How to Read a Book* (1940, revised 1972)
- **Purpose**: Multi-level reading framework for deep understanding
- **Levels**: Elementary (basic literacy), Inspectional (skimming/systematic), Analytical (deep comprehension), Syntopical (comparative across multiple books)
- **Application**: Classify book, x-ray structure, come to terms, determine author's message, criticize fairly
- **Best for**: Complex non-fiction, philosophical works, understanding full argument

### Feynman Technique
- **Source**: Richard Feynman (attributed), popularized in *Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!* (1985)
- **Purpose**: Simplification through explanation, identifying knowledge gaps
- **Process**: Choose concept, explain in simple terms, identify gaps, refine and simplify
- **Application**: Distillation of complex ideas, testing comprehension, clear explanations
- **Best for**: Summary requests, explaining concepts, teaching others

### Actionable Insight Extraction
- **Source**: Implementation science, behavioral design (e.g., BJ Fogg's Behavior Model)
- **Purpose**: Convert theoretical ideas to concrete, implementable actions
- **Framework**: Identify behavior, make it tiny, find trigger, celebrate, iterate
- **Application**: Behavior change, habit formation, implementation planning
- **Best for**: "What should I do" requests, implementation focus, behavior change

### Cornell Note-Taking System
- **Source**: Walter Pauk, *How to Study in College* (1962)
- **Purpose**: Structured capture for review and recall
- **Structure**: Cue column (keywords), Note-taking area (main content), Summary section (bottom)
- **Application**: Organized notes, efficient review, self-testing
- **Best for**: Study requests, note-taking guidance, retention focus

## Common Analysis Patterns

### Summary Structure (SQ3R-based)
1. **Survey**: Book overview, author context, main argument
2. **Question**: Key questions the book answers
3. **Read**: Main content organized by key ideas
4. **Recite**: Core concepts in own words
5. **Review**: Summary and takeaways

### Action Extraction Pattern
1. **Identify core principle**: What's the main behavior or concept?
2. **Make specific**: What exact action implements this?
3. **Find trigger**: When/where will this action occur?
4. **Plan barriers**: What could prevent success?
5. **Measure success**: How will you know it worked?

### Comparison Framework
1. **Core question**: What decision is being made?
2. **Key distinctions**: How do the books differ?
3. **Similarities**: Where do they agree?
4. **Trade-offs**: What does each prioritize?
5. **Recommendation**: Which for which situation?

## Scoring Rubric Details

### Dimension Scoring Guidelines

**Comprehension Accuracy (25%)**
- 90-100: Direct quotes, captures qualifications, no misattribution
- 75-89: Accurate core, minor simplifications
- 60-74: Generally accurate, some oversimplification
- Below 60: Significant misinterpretation

**Key-Idea Extraction (25%)**
- 90-100: All major theses, evidence mapped, structure clear
- 75-89: Most key ideas, some supporting details missed
- 60-74: Core idea present, structure incomplete
- Below 60: Misses central thesis

**Actionability (20%)**
- 90-100: Immediate specific actions, implementation guidance
- 75-89: Clear actions, some guidance needed
- 60-74: General directions, not specific
- Below 60: Vague advice, platitudes

**Structure & Clarity (15%)**
- 90-100: Clear structure, hierarchical, navigable
- 75-89: Well-organized, minor issues
- 60-74: Understandable, could be better organized
- Below 60: Confusing, disjointed

**Transfer & Application (15%)**
- 90-100: Explicit user-context connections, concrete examples
- 75-89: Clear connections, some adaptation
- 60-74: General connections, not specific
- Below 60: No user-context connection
